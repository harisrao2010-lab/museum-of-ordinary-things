A practical guide

How to keep everyday memories in a digital museum.

A memory does not need to be a milestone. A warm patch of sunlight, a familiar voice, or a small kindness can be worth keeping simply because it made an ordinary day feel like yours.

What is a digital memory museum?

A digital memory museum is a personal collection of moments you want to remember. Instead of saving only photographs or major events, you can give small details a short title and a few words of context. Think of each note as an exhibit label: enough to bring the moment back, without turning memory keeping into a chore.

1. Notice one small moment

Start with something specific from today. What made you pause? What did you hear, taste, or see? Maybe the rain arrived during tea, someone told a terrible joke, or the evening light landed on the kitchen wall. You do not need to explain why it matters to anyone else.

2. Give it a name and a short label

Choose a simple title that will help you recognize the moment later, such as “The dog’s serious meeting” or “The sun found the hallway.” Add one sentence about what happened or how it felt. A few honest words are easier to revisit than a perfect paragraph.

3. Keep a private collection at your own pace

Some people write every day; others save a memory once in a while. There is no schedule to follow. The Museum of Ordinary Things stores the exhibits you create in your browser on your device. They are not a public feed and are not sent to the site as posts. This means the collection does not automatically sync between your devices, so download a backup if you want to move it or keep a spare copy.

4. Share only what you choose

Your personal notes stay private unless you choose to share one using your browser’s share controls. Read the privacy note to see how local storage, hosting, and typefaces work. Avoid putting sensitive personal information in anything you may share.

Memory-keeping prompts

  • What small kindness do you still remember?
  • What sound would you put in a jar and keep?
  • What did the light touch today?
  • What ordinary taste belongs to this season?
  • What would you tell tomorrow-you not to forget?

Start with one moment

You do not need a special notebook, an account, or a long routine. Pick one detail from today and give it a name. Create your first exhibit in the museum; it will be saved in this browser.
